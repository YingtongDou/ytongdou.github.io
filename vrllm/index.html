01 THE RESEARCH QUESTION

Language models.
Structured decisions.

Large language models understand text. Can that knowledge translate into better predictions on industrial tabular data?

We fine-tuned Llama 3.2 1B on billions of labeled payment transactions, retaining its native tokenizer, vocabulary, and language modeling head. The result is VRLLM: the Visa Research LLM.

On our temporal holdout test set, this compact model outperforms strong tree-based, neural, and tabular foundation model baselines—and edges ahead of our purpose-built production classifier.

THE KEY IDEA

Use next-token prediction to produce a continuous classification score. A familiar language-model interface becomes a compact decision model.

Go deeper: training choices and validation+

Pretraining and semantics matter

Reinitializing attention and FFN weights substantially hurts performance. Replacing merchant names with opaque IDs also creates a persistent performance gap.

Training needs a schedule

Training begins with a batch size of 32. After approximately 100M unique records, gradient accumulation increases the effective batch size to 512, balancing stable minority-class signals with update frequency.

Inference needs consistency

Fixed input formatting, padding, GPU kernels, and software and hardware configurations help maintain deterministic scores. Business-specific probability calibration is applied before evaluation.

04 VRLLM INTERPRETATION

Inside
the decision.

A strong prediction is a starting point. We look inside VRLLM to understand the learned priors, the components it relies on, and its ability to generalize.

THE MODEL PERSPECTIVE

The first two FFN layers
make a lasting difference.

Ablating either layer 0 or layer 1 causes linear-probe accuracy to plateau early, well below the unablated model. Other individual FFN layers and attention heads have much smaller effects.

0123…15Early layers, lasting influence

The intervention supports the importance of these components for linearly decodable task information. It does not establish exactly what computation they perform.

THE DATA PERSPECTIVE

Different features.
Different kinds of prior.

Product code
Identity-specific base rates

Swapping a low-rate code for a high-rate code raises predicted probability in both classes—consistent with a learned, per-code lookup.

Dollar bucket
An ordered magnitude prior

Higher amount buckets raise predicted probability even with similar historical positive rates. Pretraining is one possible source of this prior.

Feature swaps measure model sensitivity. They can produce off-manifold inputs and do not establish real-world causal effects.

BEHIND THE RANKING

Four things the headline score cannot tell you.

01 / CALIBRATION
0.06–0.11

Better models track the base rate.

The three top-ranked models produce mean scores near the sample’s 10% positive rate. Lower-ranked models can be badly shifted: Gemini-2.5-pro has a median score of 0.965.

Perplexity-Decider-v1.2 places 99.9% of scores in the narrow interval [0.59, 0.69].

02 / SCORE RESOLUTION
266 vs. 114

A score needs room to rank.

GPT-6-Astra produces 266 distinct scores across 10K inputs; Claude-fable-5.1 produces 114. OpenAI Decisions API, Claude-sonnet-4, and Claude-opus-4.5 produce only 40–49.

Coarse score grids constrain ranking granularity, even when the underlying model can reason well.

03 / CLASS SEPARATION
0.26 / 0.09

Separating positives from negatives.

GPT-6-Astra assigns average scores of 0.26 to positive transactions and 0.09 to negatives—a gap of 0.17. Claude-fable-5.1 and Gemini-4-argon each have a 0.15 gap.

Perplexity-Decider-v1.2 separates the classes by only 0.01 (0.61 vs. 0.60).

04 / DOMAIN PRIORS
Category
+ amount

Useful knowledge before fine-tuning.

The three top-ranked models show stronger merchant-category priors and assign higher positive-class probabilities to larger-dollar transactions, echoing the amount-bucket pattern in VRLLM.

These are useful starting signals. Task-specific supervision remains necessary to capture the finer predictive patterns in this study.

ONE MORE INTRIGUING OBSERVATION

A shared pattern.
A distinctive outlier.

GPT-6-Astra, Claude-fable-5.1, Claude-opus-4.5, Kimi-K3, and Grok-4.7 form a tightly coupled prediction cluster, with rank correlations of 0.63–0.86.

Gemini-4-argon stands apart: its mean correlation with the other listed models is only 0.26; its maximum is 0.44, with GPT-6-Astra.

What makes models agree—and what makes them see differently?

Training recipes and calibration strategies are possible directions for investigation. The correlation analysis describes behavior; it does not establish the cause.

How the zero-shot benchmark was run+

We use the semantically rich serialization template on 10,000 transactions sampled from the internal temporal holdout, with a 1:9 positive-to-negative ratio. Frontier LLMs are prompted to return scores between 1 and 1,000.

Perplexity-Decider-v1.2 ↗ is hosted on-premises; its class logits are converted into probabilities. Other models are accessed through secure enterprise APIs. The OpenAI Decisions API ↗ returns a positive-outcome probability directly. Scores and probabilities are converted and calibrated before metric computation.
